# CRN-M-02 / D2 — Scope v1

## Mission

Move from the D1 idealized reading model to a safe applied understanding of how drum/sheave geometry, rope routing, spooling state and non-ideal load paths change what the mechanism actually does — without pretending that public metadata or generic mechanics provide equipment design limits.

## Prerequisite

`CRN-M-02 / D1 FROZEN v1`, tag `crn-m-02-d1-v1`.

D2 may reuse D1 terminology, rope-path tracing, force-map discipline, model-status labels and field-authority guards. D2 MUST NOT silently change D1 frozen content.

## D2 learner should be able to

1. explain why rope-drive geometry is a system variable rather than decoration;
2. distinguish ideal equal-tension reasoning from an applied system where friction, bending, alignment and contact state can make branch forces/reactions differ;
3. describe rope entry/exit and fleet/deflection angle qualitatively and identify why excessive side contact or poor tracking is an escalation signal;
4. describe single-layer, multilayer, layer-transition and crossover states and why the local rope/drum contact history matters;
5. build an applied rope-path + force/reaction map for a simplified drum/sheave arrangement;
6. identify where a change in routing or geometry can alter bearing/sheave/drum reactions even if the lifted load is unchanged;
7. distinguish a conceptual compatibility observation from a design/acceptance decision;
8. state when exact standard text, passport/OEM data or qualified inspection is required before any field action.

## In scope

- applied rope-drive geometry and load-path reasoning;
- drum groove/flange/rope-lead concepts at qualitative level;
- sheave redirection and reaction-force concepts;
- fleet/deflection angle as a qualitative geometry variable;
- single-layer vs multilayer spooling state;
- layer transitions and crossover zones;
- qualitative sources of unequal rope tension/reaction in real reeving;
- friction/bending/contact losses as concepts, not equipment efficiency numbers;
- recognition of tracking, side-contact, crushing/abrasion/crossover-risk patterns as escalation cues;
- relation of mechanism duty/classification context to engineering selection, without reproducing paid selection tables;
- documentation/authority hierarchy for actual equipment.

## Explicitly out of D2

- numeric D/d factors or minimum drum/sheave diameters;
- groove radius, pitch, depth or numerical fleet-angle limits;
- rope selection factors, design factors or breaking-force calculations from paid tables;
- numerical efficiency factors or tension-loss coefficients presented as equipment values;
- drum shell/flange stress calculations, weld design or structural verification;
- contact-pressure limits, bearing-life calculations or detailed tribology;
- fatigue-life prediction;
- discard/acceptance limits unless exact applicable source text is open and explicitly incorporated;
- NDT/MRT interpretation beyond recognition/escalation;
- redesign, repair instructions, reeving changes or equipment-specific dimensions without authorized documentation.

## Model boundary

D2 may use symbolic or qualitative mechanics to show *why* geometry/contact/friction can change reactions and invalidate D1 shortcuts.

Any formula that needs an equipment coefficient, standard factor or numeric limit must keep that term symbolic or remain concept-only unless the exact applicable source is available.

## Safety / authority boundary

D2 authorizes explanation, model selection and escalation. It does not authorize changing reeving, machining/replacing drums or sheaves, selecting a replacement rope, accepting defects, or overriding passport/OEM/mandatory requirements.
