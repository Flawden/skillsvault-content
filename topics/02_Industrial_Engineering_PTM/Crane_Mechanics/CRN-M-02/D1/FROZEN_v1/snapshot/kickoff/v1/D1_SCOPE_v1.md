# CRN-M-02 / D1 — Scope v1

## Mission

Give a beginner a mechanically correct mental model of how a hoisting rope is stored, redirected and multiplied through drums, sheaves/blocks and reeving before deeper design or diagnostic work begins.

## D1 learner should be able to

1. identify a rope drum, sheave/block, fixed and moving block, and the basic reeving path on a simplified scheme;
2. explain what each component does in the load path;
3. trace the rope from drum to dead end / moving block and count supporting rope parts;
4. explain ideal mechanical advantage as a model, not as a guarantee of real force reduction;
5. relate ideal load speed to rope speed qualitatively and with simple ratios;
6. distinguish single-layer and multilayer winding at recognition level;
7. recognize groove, flange, rope entry/exit and fleet/deflection angle as important geometry concepts without memorizing unsourced limits;
8. state when passport/OEM documentation is required before a real equipment decision.

## In scope

- function and anatomy of the drum;
- function and anatomy of rope sheaves / blocks;
- fixed vs moving blocks;
- simplified reeving schemes and supporting rope parts;
- ideal reeving ratio / mechanical advantage;
- qualitative speed-force trade-off;
- basic winding vocabulary: first layer, next layer, crossover/transition recognition;
- groove/contact vocabulary;
- rope entry/exit and angle concepts;
- why geometry affects rope life and system compatibility at a conceptual level;
- documentation hierarchy for real equipment.

## Explicitly out of D1

- numeric D/d selection factors or minimum diameters;
- groove radius, pitch, depth or fleet-angle numeric tolerances;
- drum shell/flange stress design;
- fatigue-life calculation and advanced bending mechanics;
- multilayer package mechanics, pretension models or cross-layer pressure;
- detailed efficiency/friction models;
- discard limits for drums/sheaves unless an exact applicable source is opened;
- redesign, repair instructions or permission to change reeving;
- equipment-specific dimensions, capacities or service limits without passport/OEM evidence.

## Formula boundary

Allowed in D1:

- simple ideal ratio reasoning based on the number of supporting rope parts;
- simple ideal speed/distance reciprocity used as a teaching model.

Every such formula must be labelled **idealized**. Real systems include friction, rope bending, reeving geometry and equipment-specific effects.

## Safety / authority boundary

A D1 learner may explain and trace a system. D1 does not authorize changing reeving, selecting replacement dimensions, accepting defects, altering drum/sheave geometry or overriding the manufacturer/passport/mandatory rules.
