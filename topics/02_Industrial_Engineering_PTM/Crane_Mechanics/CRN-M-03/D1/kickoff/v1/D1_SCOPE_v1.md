# CRN-M-03 / D1 — Scope v1

## Mission

Give a beginner a mechanically and operationally safe mental model of how a crane hook suspension and common load-gripping / below-hook devices connect the hoisting system to the load, before inspection criteria, design proof, device-specific capacity checks or field decisions are introduced.

## D1 learner should be able to

1. distinguish the crane-integral hook suspension / hook block from a detachable below-hook lifting attachment;
2. identify generic hook features where applicable: body, point/tip, throat/opening, saddle/bowl, shank or eye, and a separate keeper/latch when present;
3. trace the conceptual load path from rope/sheaves or suspension structure through the block/frame/crosshead interface into the hook and then into the load;
4. explain that the hook body, suspension interfaces and any keeper/latch do not automatically have the same structural role;
5. recognize correct-load-seating and alignment concepts and explain why tip loading, side loading or unintended eccentric loading can change the force path, without inventing numeric limits;
6. classify common load-handling principles at recognition level: positive mechanical engagement, clamps/grips, lifting beams/C-hooks/forks, magnets, vacuum devices, and grab/grab-bucket mechanisms;
7. distinguish a device that positively captures a load from a device whose holding force depends on friction, magnetic force, vacuum, geometry, control state or another mechanism-specific condition;
8. recognize that the attachment/device self-weight and interface compatibility belong to the real lifting-system decision, while exact capacity calculations remain source- and equipment-dependent;
9. recognize common failure families at concept level: loss of engagement, unintended opening/release, misalignment, deformation/damage, wear/cracking indicators, control/energy loss for powered devices, and loss of holding condition;
10. state when exact standard text, passport/OEM documentation, device documentation or competent specialist/authority is required before a real equipment decision.

## In scope

- hook suspension / hook-block role in the hoisting load path;
- generic hook nomenclature and anatomy at recognition level;
- body / shank or eye / saddle / point / throat vocabulary where applicable;
- keeper/latch as a distinct functional element, without assuming it is load-bearing;
- qualitative seating, alignment, eccentricity, side-load and tip-load concepts;
- distinction between integral crane load-handling components and detachable load-lifting attachments;
- conceptual categories of clamps/grips, C-hooks, forks, lifting beams, magnets, vacuum devices, grabs and grab buckets;
- positive engagement versus holding-force-dependent gripping principles;
- attachment self-weight and compatibility as concepts, not unsourced calculations;
- observation-to-source/OEM escalation for visible damage, uncertain configuration or unclear device applicability;
- documentation hierarchy for real equipment and attachments.

## Explicitly out of D1

- numeric hook dimensions, throat limits, wear/deformation limits or discard criteria;
- material grades, allowable stresses, fatigue factors, design factors or proof-of-competence calculations;
- exact side-load/tip-load limits or permitted eccentricity;
- hook-nut/thread/bearing/retainer dimensions, preload or assembly criteria;
- latch/keeper design, retrofit or acceptance criteria;
- load-test values or test procedures presented as field instructions;
- magnetic, vacuum, friction-clamp or grab holding-force calculations and safety factors;
- grab/grab-bucket design, closure-force, penetration, capacity or stability calculations;
- specialist NDT interpretation, repair welding, heat treatment or refurbishment instructions;
- equipment-specific rated-capacity deductions or device compatibility decisions without the applicable documentation;
- permission to continue operation, repair, modify or return a device to service.

## Mechanics boundary

Allowed in D1:

- qualitative load-path arrows;
- simple free-body sketches used only to show where force is transmitted;
- qualitative comparison of centered versus obviously eccentric / side-loaded paths;
- symbolic recognition that a below-hook device can add self-weight to the lifted system.

D1 does **not** turn these ideas into a sizing, proof, inspection-acceptance or operational-permission calculation.

## Safety / authority boundary

A D1 learner may identify components, trace a load path, classify a gripping principle and recognize when the configuration is uncertain or unsafe to reason about from appearance alone.

D1 does not authorize selecting a replacement hook/device, approving a latch, judging a crack/wear limit, changing a hook suspension, modifying a grab/clamp, performing repair, accepting a test result or permitting continued operation.
